BUSHFIRE SAFETY × AI

Embers from powerline faults can start deadly bushfires.So I trained an AI to catch them.

On Black Saturday 2009, a powerline started the Kilmore East fire: 119 lives, $494 million. My AI finds embers too small and fast for the eye, follows each one, and measures its speed, path and lifespan.

THE PROBLEM

The protection can’t see it

When a branch touches a live powerline, only a tiny current flows at first: far too little to trip the protection. So the branch keeps burning, and the whole time it sheds embers that can start a fire on the ground.

HOW IT WORKS

Teaching a computer to see embers

The videos come from a Victorian Government test program that staged over a thousand of these faults on a 22 kV test rig. I analysed 100 tests in depth, 93 of them in the final results, covering 11 tree species.

STEP 1

I marked 3,755 embers by hand

In 445 video frames, about 13 hours of work, to show the computer what an ember looks like.

STEP 2

An AI learned to spot them

A U-Net neural network checks every pixel. Its trick: it also sees what got brighter in the half-second before and after, so moving embers stand out from the steady flame.

STEP 3

It follows each ember

Frame by frame, measuring how fast each ember moves, which way it goes, where it starts and how long it lasts.

OFF ≠ safe

embers keep falling after the power is cut

The branch is still burning, so protection that acts late can’t take them back.

THE SURPRISE

More embers≠ more danger

Each dot is a tree species. Willow fits the obvious rule: the most embers and the highest fire risk. But the rule doesn’t hold.

Desert Ash sheds the fewest embers, yet ranks second for fire risk, most likely because its slow faults give a few large embers time to reach the ground. Golden Wattle sheds plenty and is low risk.

It’s not how many embers. It’s which ones, and when.

Protection needs to be fast, not just sensitive.

Embers released = ember rate × time to trip. Cut the time and you cut the embers. This work gives the first per-ember numbers to design for, and the pipeline is ready to run on the remaining 800 or so test videos.
